## From AL champions to the bottom of the division

The Blue Jays went 79-83 in 2026: fifth in the AL East, 19 games back, and 15 wins short of 2025's 94-68. The pitching staff allowed 27 fewer runs than the year before, but the lineup scored 150 fewer, so the run differential fell from +77 to −46.

In other words, almost the whole collapse came from the offense. The small gain in run prevention offset less than a fifth of the runs the lineup lost.

## The lineup: plenty of contact, not much carry

The problem wasn't swing-and-miss. It was that the ball didn't go anywhere. The Jays' 19.0% strikeout rate was the second-lowest in MLB, but their ISO (.134) and Hard-hit% (34.0%) both ranked 30th.

The result was 4.00 runs per game, the fewest in the majors. Added up over the player table, home runs fell from 191 to 152, and Off (batting plus baserunning) dropped from +84.1 runs to −43.4, a swing of about 128 runs.

This was a lineup that put the ball in play but rarely brought a run home with one swing. It needed three or four hits in a row to score, and when the hits didn't string together, the inning came up empty.

## Power is down everywhere, most of all in the outfield

Outfielders' home runs fell from 69 to 41. That 28-homer drop is the biggest at any position: about 70% of the team's net loss of 39.

<PositionValue season={2026} metric="hr" />

Measured by overall run production (Off), though, shortstop, DH and first base each lost about 30 runs: shortstop is the hole Bichette left, DH and first base are Springer and Guerrero Jr.

<PositionValue season={2026} metric="off" />

The outfield's problem was a lack of home-run power. In 2025 the outfield's home runs came from Varsho (20), Lukes (11), Barger (10 while playing the outfield, 21 in all) and Schneider (9). In 2026 Barger got only 28 plate appearances, Varsho went from 20 home runs to 7, and Lukes was the only outfielder to reach double digits.

The fall-off in the middle of the order hurt even more. Springer's Off dropped from +48.3 to +4.0 and Guerrero Jr.'s from +27.0 to −5.4. Together that is about 77 runs, roughly 60% of the team's decline.

Second base sat around −13 runs of Off in both seasons with no improvement, mostly Clement in 2026 (−14.4 for the season). The one bright spot was newcomer Okamoto, who hit 33 home runs on his own and took third base's total from 15 to 33.

## Pitching: Cease and the bullpen held, the back of the rotation didn't

Runs allowed fell from 721 to 694 (10th in MLB). The staff's 3.97 FIP ranked 8th and its 1.05 HR/9 ranked 7th. But the improvement came from a handful of pitchers.

<TeamStats season={2026} block="prevention" highlight={["ra_per_g", "fip", "hr9"]} />

Cease was the find of the season: a 34.9% strikeout rate and 5.5 WAR, the best on the team. Varland went from a supporting role in 2025 to closer, and with Miles and Rogers the back of the bullpen was solid.

The four starters other than Cease and Yesavage (Gausman, Scherzer, Corbin and Soriano) threw about 346 innings with an innings-weighted ERA of about 5.12. In 2025, the four in those spots (Gausman, Bassitt, Berríos and Lauer) had ERAs between 3.18 and 4.17. The rotation's depth went backwards.

Two caveats:

- Rogers' 1.77 ERA sits far below his 3.35 FIP. Some of that is probably luck, and it may not repeat next year.
- Run prevention includes the defense. Giménez was worth +19 OAA and +20.1 Def, and Kirk and Valenzuela were both above +13 Def, so not all of the 27 runs saved belong to the pitchers.

## The home edge disappeared; the pitching kept the second half alive

Of the 15 wins lost from 2025, 12 were lost at home.

The road record was about the same as the year before; nearly the whole gap was at Rogers Centre. Per game, run prevention didn't improve in a straight line. May was the best month, June and July bounced back up to nearly 5 runs allowed per game, and only from August on did it settle at about 4. The offense, meanwhile, sat between 3.7 and 4.2 runs per game all year.

August (17-11) was the best month of the year, with a +8 run differential. September's run differential was still +4, yet the record was only 11-13, right when the wild-card race mattered most.

## How the wild-card race ended, and the open questions for 2027

The Jays hung around the edge of the wild-card race into late September and were eliminated with a loss to the Orioles on September 23. The pitching and defense kept them in it that long; a lineup that still couldn't score in a decisive September is why they fell short. 😞

Seen another way, the run differential points to a 76-86 team. The actual record was 79-83, so the Jays had already won three more games than expected, yet they went 20-24 in one-run games. On the surface those two numbers contradict each other, and they deserve a closer look.

The questions for 2027 come down to three:

1. Where does outfield power come from? Only Lukes reached 10 home runs in 2026; Bateman hit well but had just 2 home runs in 181 plate appearances.
2. Is Guerrero Jr.'s season a one-year dip or a trend? He fell from a 137 wRC+ to 95, the single biggest bounce-back question on the roster.
3. The rotation after Cease. Gausman's ERA rose from 3.59 to 4.38, and Scherzer, Corbin and Soriano were all above 5.

We'll dig into these in the articles to come!

Here's hoping the 2027 Blue Jays make it back to the World Series!
