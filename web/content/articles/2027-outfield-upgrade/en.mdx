## The gap: an outfield that can't hit it out

The 2026 Blue Jays outfield could still hit. It just couldn't hit the ball far. Its OPS was .707, only a little below the average MLB outfield (.723) and 19th of 30 clubs. But it hit just 41 home runs against a league average of 58 per club, tied for 29th.

DH was an even bigger hole: a .644 OPS, 28th. So the best-value move this winter is a slugger who can play a corner outfield spot and also DH, one player filling two holes. (George, I still love you 💙)

After Varsho left 🥲, Straw and Bateman covered center field. Straw's glove is steady, but his bat never got going, and the Jays have to decide after the World Series whether to pick up his $8MM option (declining it costs a $1.75MM buyout). Anthony Santander isn't in the table. He tore the labrum in his left shoulder before Opening Day, didn't play a big-league game all season and only started a rehab assignment in Buffalo on September 9. Healthy, he's a 20-homer bat who can play right field.

DH needs help too. George Springer (37 in 2027) is a free agent after a 105 wRC+ and 15 home runs in 517 plate appearances this year. If he leaves, the DH hole gets bigger 😭

Now the free-agent market. Only three of this winter's free-agent outfielders can fill the 20-homer gap on their own: two steady 32-year-old sluggers, and one unknown from Japan.

**Randy Arozarena**: his sixth straight 20-homer, 20-steal season, and 2026 was the best of his career (151 wRC+, 5.5 WAR). The knocks are age and defense: Def -11.7 and OAA -5 in left field (OAA counts the outs a fielder makes above or below an average one). For the Jays he could play left now and slide to DH over the later years of a deal.

**Seiya Suzuki**: his fourth straight season of 20-plus homers (32 in 2025), with a .368 OBP. He doesn't run like Arozarena, but his right-field defense grades out positive (Def +1.5, OAA +5). A slightly lesser bat than Arozarena, and very close overall.

**Teruaki Sato**: expected to be posted by the Hanshin Tigers. He hit .306 / .383 / .609 with 39 home runs in 614 plate appearances in NPB this year (40 the year before) and is a front-runner for Central League MVP. He's the youngest of the three, has the most power and carries the most risk: there are questions about his hit tool, he struck out in 26.7% of his plate appearances (164 in 614), and he has played more third base than right field in recent seasons. NPB numbers don't translate one-to-one to MLB.

Sato comes with two more question marks. NPB is a harder league to hit home runs in, so his 39 are worth more than they look, but the recent NPB sluggers who came over all struck out a lot more in MLB. And he has mostly played third base lately, so for a defense-first club like the Jays he looks more like a third base or DH option. I'll come back to both in a separate piece comparing him with other NPB sluggers.

> Arozarena and Suzuki will probably both receive and turn down a qualifying offer. Signing either would cost the Jays a draft pick.

## The second tier and the cheaper options

If the Jays don't want to spend big, the market has four other kinds of player: full-time bats who don't field well, platoon bats who mash one side, high-risk, high-upside rebound bets, and center fielders who can catch everything but don't hit.

The Jays have leaned on defense for years, though. Their team OAA ranked in the top 12 of 30 clubs in all five seasons from 2022 to 2026, including tied for 5th in 2024 and 7th in 2025. Trading for Varsho and signing Kevin Kiermaier before 2023 were glove-first moves too. So read the Def column alongside the bat: players with clearly negative defense, like Bauers (-14.5) and Conforto (-8.2), look more like DHs than everyday outfielders in Toronto.

**Full-time bats**: Jake Bauers was the surprise of the year, and he just won NLCS MVP too 🫡. ## Timing: December 1 is the line

The most important date of this offseason is December 1, when the collective bargaining agreement expires. The big signings will likely crowd in before it.

1. **A few days after the World Series: options, free-agent filing and qualifying offers.** Clubs decide on options such as Straw's ($8MM) and Robert Jr.'s ($20MM), and top free agents such as Arozarena and Suzuki may get qualifying offers.
2. **November 1 – December 15: the NPB posting window.** If Hanshin posts Sato, he gets 45 days to negotiate with MLB clubs.
3. **December 1: the CBA expires (11:59 p.m. ET).** Most reports expect the owners to lock the players out. In 2021 the Jays signed Gausman a few days before the last lockout.
4. **During a lockout: signings and trades involving 40-man rosters freeze.** The last lockout ran from December 2, 2021 to March 10, 2022, and paused Suzuki's posting window too.
5. **After a new agreement: the market reopens.** The unsigned free agents sign in a rush before spring training.

If there is a lockout, Sato's posting window could be paused the way Suzuki's was in 2021, and the Jays' outfield fix might not land until just before spring training.

## Bottom line: sign one slugger, let the farm supply the depth

The outfield is short on home runs, and the farm can't produce a 20-homer bat next year, so the fix has to come from free agency. Three paths, and their trade-offs:

1. **The big contract**: Arozarena or Suzuki. Given how much the Jays value defense, Suzuki (Def +1.5) fits better than Arozarena (Def -11.7). Both can play a corner and move to DH as they age, filling the outfield power gap and the DH spot Springer leaves behind. The cost is a multi-year deal for a 32-year-old, plus a draft pick for the qualifying offer. Sato is the youngest and has the most power, but the jump from NPB carries the most risk: his strikeouts could climb sharply, and he may not end up in the outfield.
2. **A platoon**: Grichuk against lefties, Conforto against righties, rotating between a corner spot and DH. Together they had about 600 plate appearances and 30 home runs this year, as much as one full-time slugger; the risks are age (35 and 34) and defense (both negative Def).
3. **The farm and in-house options**: Piñango, Schreck and Bateman as the fourth and fifth outfielders, with Jake Cook as the longer-term answer in center. A healthy Santander and Barger would be power that costs nothing extra, but they can't be the whole plan.

My take: one big contract at most, then wait on the players coming back and the farm's young players. Sign one top-tier corner bat, and I think Suzuki fits best; give center field to Bateman and Straw (if the Jays pick up his option); and once Barger and Santander are healthy again, let the farm cover the rest of the depth.

In 2025 we were at the World Series; in 2026 the season ended early. How the Jays fill the outfield this winter is the first step back to October 🤞
