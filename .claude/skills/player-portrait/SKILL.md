# Player portraits

A player drawn in the site's own hand: one head for everyone (a plain cap, jersey with a piped V-neck, a
light steel disc inside the site frames' rough.js double ring), only the features changing, so a set of
portraits reads as one illustrated series. The look is a loose ink illustration, after a reference the
author supplied (2026-10-22): brush lines built as filled shapes (pressure, a quick start, a thin flick at
the end, overshoot, the odd break and a lighter second pass), flat colour patches nudged off the lines,
skin left mostly paper (lighter skin) or one flat base (darker skin) with the shadow side blocked in, and
stubble, beards and sideburns as short pen strokes.

Turned down on the way (don't go back to them): an engraving traced from the MLB headshot (a photo
derivative), then a clean vector cartoon with even rough.js lines ("looks drawn with D3", too "American
cartoon"). Kept from that cartoon version at the author's request: the light steel background and the
rough.js double ring. Tried and removed: the SN monogram on the cap, and a terracotta background.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `web/content/portraits/<id>.json` | the features, written by hand (this skill) |
| `web/content/portraits/<id>.svg` | the drawing, generated by `web/scripts/player-portrait.mjs`: never hand-edit it |
| `web/scripts/player-portrait.mjs` | the one head and the hand (`brush` / `wash` / `hatch`): `FEATURES` documents every field and its range |

Cost: looking at the headshot once and at the result once or twice, plus a ~15-line JSON. Drawing is a
local script that takes a second; no image model is involved. The SVG is 150–350 KB (every brush line is a
filled shape); the share cards embed it as is.

## Ground rules

- **On demand, one player at a time.** Draw a player when a card needs him; never batch the roster ahead.
- **Features, not a likeness.** Look at his official MLB headshot only to read the features; nothing from
  the photo goes into the drawing. Never use game photos (Getty, AP, Imagn, team or news sites).
- **Caricature = exaggerate what's distinctive, leave the rest at 1.** Vladimir Guerrero Jr. is a round,
  wide face (`width` 1.14), a full beard and a huge grin (`mouth.width` 1.5); George Springer a lean, longer
  face (`width` 0.95, `jaw` 0.86, `length` 1.08), stubble, big ears (1.12) and a chain. If two players come
  out looking alike, push their differences further.
- **Skin tone from the photo, respectfully:** pick the nearest of `fair | light | tan | brown | deep`; never
  exaggerate skin tone or any ethnic feature for effect. Caricature is for face shape, beard, grin, ears.
- **The cap stays plain: no logo.** The author tried the SN monogram there and took it off; the club's bird
  and maple leaf are trademarks anyway. A player now with another club gets the same cap; the card's text
  says the club.
- **The style lives only in `player-portrait.mjs`.** If a drawing looks wrong, first fix the features; if the
  head itself is wrong (the cap, the eyes, line weight, how far the washes sit off the lines), fix the script
  and redraw every portrait, so the series stays one style. Layers, back to front: washes, body / face /
  beard ink, the features (mouth, eyes, brows), the cap. Colours are token mixes: keep the hexes in sync with
  `web/app/globals.css`. The noise is seeded by the id, so a redraw is identical unless something changed.

## Workflow

1. **Find the id.** `web_players.mlbam_id`, or the player's site URL `/players/<id>`. Check name ↔ id.
2. **Look at the headshot** (save it to the scratchpad, open it with `Read`):
   `https://img.mlbstatic.com/mlb-photos/image/upload/w_400,q_auto:best/v1/people/<id>/headshot/silo/current`
   (404 = MLB has no photo: ask the author for another reference). Note what someone would draw first:
   face shape, beard style, skin tone, ears, nose width, grin, brows, chain.
3. **Write `web/content/portraits/<id>.json`** with `name` and only the fields that differ from 1 / the
   defaults (see `FEATURES` in the script; the two existing files are examples).
4. **Draw and check**, from `web/`:
   ```bash
   node scripts/player-portrait.mjs <id> --png <scratchpad>/portraits
   ```
   Open the PNG once. Is it recognisably him next to the other portraits? Adjust the JSON and redraw;
   stop after two rounds and show the author rather than polishing alone.
5. **Use it.** In a card spec, a fact names him: `{ "label": …, "value": "+48.3 → +4.0", "player": 543807 }`.
   Part cards draw it (`PlayerTile` in `web/lib/share-card/card.tsx`): on Instagram two player facts sit
   side by side with big roundels, on X a small roundel left of the value. Render with `/share-card`.
   News cards don't draw portraits yet: that is a `NewsBody` change for every news card, not one.
